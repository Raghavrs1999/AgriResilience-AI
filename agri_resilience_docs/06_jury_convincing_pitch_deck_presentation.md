## Judy's "Grill-Me" Session: Anticipated Tough Jury Questions

### Question 1: "Why can't farmers just use WhatsApp with ChatGPT or Plantix?"
**Winning Answer**:
> *"Neither WhatsApp nor Plantix have access to planetary satellite radar. A photo alone cannot tell you if a storm is arriving in 3 hours, nor can it calculate vegetative NDVI chlorophyll decay. If a farmer asks ChatGPT what to spray, it won't check if the rain will wash $40 of chemicals into the river 2 hours later. Plantix is also commercially compromised by pesticide dealer sponsorships and locked behind English text menus. AgriResilience is voice-first, environmentally aware, and financially protects the farmer."*

### Question 2: "What happens when monsoons block optical satellites for weeks?"
**Winning Answer**:
> *"Our Earth Engine pipeline includes an automated 14-day rolling cloud-mask lookback. When a satellite pass has >30% cloud cover, the system automatically walks back day-by-day to retrieve the most recent cloud-free pixel baseline. Crucially, our weather radar uses ERA5 atmospheric reanalysis and microwave radar, which penetrates dense cloud cover effortlessly to provide 100% reliable precipitation forecasts."*

### Question 3: "How do you handle illiterate farmers who mix local dialects with English?"
**Winning Answer**:
> *"Traditional ASR pipelines fail on rural code-switching (e.g., Hinglish or Bahasa Jawa) because speech-to-text models try to force rural dialect words into English or standard Hindi dictionaries. Because we stream raw 16kHz audio directly into Gemini's multimodal audio buffer without an intermediate text transcription layer, Gemini's acoustic embeddings recognize regional agronomic jargon ('Tikka Rog', 'Karpa', 'Wereng') natively in context."*

### Question 4: "If the app is free for farmers, how do you pay for Google Cloud Run and Gemini API costs?"
**Winning Answer**:
> *"Through high-value institutional B2G contracts and InsurTech API fees. Government agriculture departments in India and Southeast Asia spend hundreds of millions annually sending surveyors into fields after crops have already failed. Governments pay per-district API subscriptions to access our BigQuery regional disease heatmaps for proactive intervention. Parametric crop insurers also pay API licensing fees to verify claims instantly through our 5km outbreak cluster telemetry, saving them up to 40% in claims adjustment overhead."*
